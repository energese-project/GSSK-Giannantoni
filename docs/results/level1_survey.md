# Level 1 survey: how often is an Odum graph in exponential form?

Generated by `python3 -I scripts/level1_survey.py` (`make level1-survey`), and checked against a fresh run in CI (`make check-level1`, T-BRG-02). Do not edit by hand.

## The numbers

- **23** kernel examples (all but `invalid_model`, the kernel's deliberately unloadable fixture). **5** of them are carried whole by the projection.
- **11** are in exponential form: every pathway law is linear in the state, and no pathway rate is forced. **3** are both in exponential form and carried whole.
- The other **18** need a law the projection does not carry, or a forcing no generator carries. They are listed in the table below, and the coverage report names each blocker (`bin/gia_bridge <model>`).

## The table

For a model carried whole, the four right-hand columns give the kernel's largest **scaled** difference from the Giannantoni engine's `exp(A t)` at the model's own `dt`: `|kernel − exact|` over the node's largest magnitude in the run, maximised over nodes and steps. The first is under the model's own method, then RK4, Euler and the kernel's own Padé `expm`.

These are classical numbers: the kernel integrator's error against the exact exponential. They are not incipient drift; both engines' trajectories are classical (PLAN.md §2 E3).

## Reading it

- **Not in exponential form, but carried whole** (`supply_chain_30`, `three_input_gate_model`). Their interaction or subtract laws are not linear in the state. The two engines then agree only as closely as both solve the same nonlinear system, so the difference is no longer one integrator's error.
- **The kernel's `expm` is exact only when the system is autonomous.** It holds a source's forcing fixed across each step. The bridge's sine-forced fixture (`tests/fixtures/bridge_sine_source.json`, not an example) shows the cost: about 0.4% at `dt = 0.5`, where RK4 is at about 1e-9.
